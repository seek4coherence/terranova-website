What Makes Lexington Different

Fayette County has an urban service boundary, and that line does more to shape property values than almost anything else in the market. Inside the boundary, land is scarce and infill lots trade at a premium. Outside it, large tracts are plentiful but often agricultural, and the buyer pool is completely different.

That matters if you own an older house near downtown, in Kenwick, or along the North Limestone corridor. The structure may be tired, but the lot is in a location where new construction is viable. A cash buyer who only looks at the house will lowball you. A buyer who understands the lot will price it correctly.

It also matters if you own acreage in Scott, Woodford, or Bourbon County. We are usually not the right buyer for a large farm. We will tell you that up front rather than waste your time.

Who Calls Us in Lexington

  • Inherited property near the university. Heirs who live out of state and do not want to manage a student rental or a house full of decades of belongings.
  • Tired landlords. Owners of small multi-family and student rentals who are done with turnover, damage, and summer vacancy.
  • Older homes with deferred maintenance. Roofs, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, and foundations that a retail buyer will want repaired before closing.
  • Estate and probate sales. Properties that need to be liquidated on a court timeline rather than a listing timeline.
  • Infill lot owners. Families holding a vacant parcel inside the boundary who have been paying taxes on it for years.

How the Offer Works

You send the address and a few details. We pull the Fayette County property record, look at recent sales on the block, estimate the repair scope, and check title. Then we send a written offer with the price, the closing costs we cover, and a timeline.

There is no obligation to accept, no fee to see the number, and no pressure to sign on the call. If the offer is not right for you, the honest answer is to list the property with an agent, and we will say so.

Cash Sale vs. Listing in Lexington

Selling to Terra Nova

  • Offer in about 24 hours
  • Close in 7 to 21 days, or later if you choose
  • No repairs, cleaning, or showings
  • No commissions or closing costs on your side
  • No financing or appraisal contingency

Listing With an Agent

  • Weeks to prepare, list, and find a buyer
  • 30 to 60 days to close after going under contract
  • Repairs and staging often required
  • 5 to 6 percent commission plus closing costs
  • Deals can fail on appraisal or financing

In a strong Lexington submarket, a well-presented house will usually net more through an agent. A cash offer wins when the house needs work, the timeline is short, or the estate cannot absorb a deal falling through. We will tell you which one fits.
