Sell Vacant Land for Cash

Land is one of the hardest things to sell in real estate. Most buyers need a mortgage, most banks will not lend on raw land, and a listing can sit for years while you keep paying taxes on it. We buy land directly, for cash, in all 50 states.

Why Land Sits on the Market

A house has a buyer pool of anyone who wants to live there. A vacant lot has a buyer pool of people who want to build, plus investors, plus the occasional neighbor. That is a much smaller group, and most of them need financing that a bank will not provide for raw land.

On top of that, land has carrying costs with no income. You pay property taxes every year, you may pay for mowing or code compliance, and you get nothing back until it sells. For an out-of-state owner who inherited a parcel they have never seen, that is a slow leak that can last a decade.

We buy land because we have a use for it. We acquire lots that fit new home construction, and we hold or improve the ones that need more work. That gives us a reason to buy parcels that a retail buyer would scroll past.

Types of Land We Buy

  • Residential infill lots. Single lots in established neighborhoods, including ones with older homes around them.
  • Rural acreage. Five to several hundred acres, with or without road frontage.
  • Inherited and estate land. Parcels split among heirs, often out of state, often with unclear title.
  • Tax-delinquent parcels. Land with years of unpaid taxes or an active tax sale.
  • Landlocked parcels. No legal access, or access only by easement.
  • Unbuildable or restricted land. Wetlands, flood zones, steep slopes, and deed-restricted parcels.
  • Builder-ready lots. Platted lots in subdivisions and growth corridors with utilities available.
  • Agricultural and transitional land. Farmland near expanding metros, and land in the path of development.

What We Look At When We Price Land

Land valuation is not the same as house valuation. There is no square footage to compare and no repair estimate. Instead we look at:

  • Access. Legal road frontage or a recorded easement. Landlocked parcels are worth dramatically less.
  • Zoning and use. What can legally be built, and whether that matches current demand.
  • Utilities. Water, sewer, electric, and gas at the street, nearby, or absent entirely.
  • Topography and drainage. Floodplain, wetlands, slope, and soil conditions.
  • Comparable land sales. Recent vacant land sales in the same area, not home sales.
  • Carrying cost. Taxes, association dues, and any maintenance obligations.

If you have a survey, a plat map, or a title report, send it. It speeds up the offer and usually improves the number because we are not pricing uncertainty.

Back Taxes, Liens, and Heirs

Land problems are usually paperwork problems, and paperwork problems are solvable. We regularly buy parcels with unpaid property taxes, old liens, missing heirs, and title that has not been cleared in decades.

In most cases the title company resolves these at closing using the sale proceeds. If probate is still open, we can structure the contract to close after the court approves the sale. If a tax sale is already scheduled, tell us the date, because the timeline changes what is possible.

How the Process Works

  1. Send the parcel details. Address or parcel ID, acreage, and what you know about access and utilities.
  2. We research it. County records, zoning, comparable land sales, and any title issues we can see.
  3. You get a written offer. Usually within a few business days, since land research takes longer than a house.
  4. Accept and close. Title company handles the deed and payoff. You do not need to travel to the property.

You never need to visit the land, clean it, or improve it. Most of our land sellers have never set foot on the parcel.

Land We Buy vs. Land We Pass On

Strong Fit

  • Residential lots in growing metros
  • Parcels with road frontage and utilities nearby
  • Zoning that allows single-family homes
  • Inherited land with clear or clearable title
  • Tax-delinquent parcels with a workable timeline

Usually a Pass

  • Parcels with no legal access at all
  • Land in a designated wetland with no buildable area
  • Fractional undivided interests with dozens of heirs
  • Land with environmental contamination
  • Parcels where the taxes exceed the land value

Even on a pass, we will tell you why. A clear no is more useful than a slow maybe.
